Quiz 1: Methodology

Test your understanding of economic methodology, opportunity cost, and correlation vs. causation

1. Which of the following is a positive statement?
✅ Correct Answer: B
"Higher taxes reduce consumer spending" is a positive statement because it can be tested with data. Statements A, C, and D contain value judgments (normative statements).

Key distinction: Positive = testable and factual; Normative = value-based and opinionated.
2. What is the opportunity cost of attending university?
✅ Correct Answer: D
Opportunity cost includes BOTH explicit costs (tuition, books) AND implicit costs (the forgone salary you could have earned). The true cost includes everything you give up.

Formula: Economic Cost = Explicit Costs + Implicit Costs
3. A study finds a correlation between ice cream sales and drowning deaths. What is the most likely explanation?
✅ Correct Answer: C
This is the classic example of a spurious correlation. Hot weather causes both ice cream sales and drowning deaths to increase. Correlation does NOT imply causation!

Lesson: Always look for third variables that might explain the relationship.
4. Which of the following is a microeconomics question?
✅ Correct Answer: A
Microeconomics focuses on individual agents and specific markets. Avocado prices are a specific market question. B, C, and D are macroeconomics questions about the whole economy.

Memory trick: Micro = individual trees; Macro = whole forest.
5. What is the opportunity cost of attending a concert if the ticket costs $50 and you could have worked 4 hours at $15/hour during that time?
✅ Correct Answer: C
Opportunity cost = Explicit cost ($50 ticket) + Implicit cost (4 hours × $15/hour = $60) = $110

Formula: Economic Cost = Explicit Costs + Implicit Costs

Quiz 2: The Market

Test your understanding of supply, demand, and market equilibrium

6. If the price of a complement increases, what happens to demand for the original good?
✅ Correct Answer: B
Complements are goods used together (coffee & sugar, cars & gasoline). When the price of a complement increases, demand for the other good decreases (shifts LEFT).

Example: If the price of printers increases, demand for ink cartridges decreases.
7. A decrease in the price of a good causes:
✅ Correct Answer: C
A change in the good's own price causes a movement along the demand curve (change in quantity demanded), NOT a shift of the curve.

Rule: Price changes cause movements; other factors cause shifts.
8. Given QD = 200 − 8P and QS = 40 + 2P, find the equilibrium price.
✅ Correct Answer: B
Set QD = QS:
200 − 8P = 40 + 2P
200 − 40 = 2P + 8P
160 = 10P
P* = 16
9. If income increases and a good is normal, what happens to demand?
✅ Correct Answer: A
For a normal good, an increase in income leads to an increase in demand (shift RIGHT).

Examples of normal goods: Restaurant meals, vacations, new cars.
Examples of inferior goods: Public transit, instant noodles, second-hand goods.
10. What is the "short side of the market" rule?
✅ Correct Answer: B
The short side of the market rule states that in disequilibrium, the actual quantity exchanged is the smaller of quantity demanded and quantity supplied.

Example: In a shortage (QD > QS), the quantity traded is QS because sellers control how much they supply.

Quiz 3: Elasticity

Test your understanding of elasticity and surplus

11. The price of a good increases from $10 to $12, and quantity demanded falls from 100 to 80. What is the own-price elasticity of demand?
✅ Correct Answer: C
%ΔQ = (80-100)/100 = -20%
%ΔP = (12-10)/10 = 20%
ε = -20%/20% = -1.0

Interpretation: Demand is unit-elastic. A 1% increase in price leads to exactly a 1% decrease in quantity demanded.
12. If demand is elastic (|ε| > 1), what happens to total revenue when price increases?
✅ Correct Answer: B
When demand is elastic, the quantity effect dominates the price effect. The percentage decrease in quantity is larger than the percentage increase in price, so total revenue decreases.

Rule: Elastic demand → Price ↑ → Revenue ↓
13. The price of Coke increases by 10%, and demand for Pepsi increases by 15%. What is the cross-price elasticity?
✅ Correct Answer: B
ε = %ΔQPepsi / %ΔPCoke = 15%/10% = 1.5
Positive elasticity → Substitutes

Rule: Positive = Substitutes; Negative = Complements
14. Income increases by 5%, and demand for restaurant meals increases by 15%. What is the income elasticity?
✅ Correct Answer: B
εR = %ΔQ / %ΔR = 15%/5% = 3.0
Since ε > 1, restaurant meals are a luxury normal good.

Rule: ε > 1 = Luxury; 0 < ε < 1 = Necessity; ε < 0 = Inferior
15. Given QD = 100 − 2P and QS = 20 + 2P, find consumer surplus.
✅ Correct Answer: A
Step 1: Find equilibrium: 100 − 2P = 20 + 2P → 80 = 4P → P* = 20, Q* = 60
Step 2: Find demand intercept: Q=0 → P = 50
Step 3: CS = ½ × Q* × (Pmax − P*) = ½ × 60 × (50 − 20) = 400

Quiz 4: Government Interventions

Test your understanding of taxes, subsidies, and price controls

16. A price ceiling set below equilibrium creates:
✅ Correct Answer: B
A price ceiling set below equilibrium creates a shortage (excess demand) because quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied.

Memory trick: Ceiling below = Shortage (think: low ceiling means less room to breathe!)
17. A price floor set above equilibrium creates:
✅ Correct Answer: B
A price floor set above equilibrium creates a surplus (excess supply) because quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.

Example: Minimum wage → surplus of labor (unemployment).
18. The more inelastic side of the market bears:
✅ Correct Answer: B
The more inelastic side bears more of the tax burden because they can't easily adjust their behavior.

Rule: The inelastic side gets "crushed" by the tax burden.
19. A subsidy is a:
✅ Correct Answer: B
A subsidy is a negative tax. It creates a wedge where sellers receive more than buyers pay (PS > PB), encouraging more production and consumption.

Effect: Quantity increases, government pays.
20. Deadweight loss from a tax is:
✅ Correct Answer: B
Deadweight loss is the lost surplus from foregone trades that do not occur because of the tax. It represents the net loss in total surplus.

Key distinction: Tax revenue is a transfer; DWL is the true loss to society.

Quiz 5: Monopoly & Game Theory

Test your understanding of market power and strategic interaction

21. A monopolist maximizes profit where:
✅ Correct Answer: B
All firms maximize profit where MR = MC. In monopoly, since MR < P, this means P > MC.

Key distinction: Perfect competition: P = MC; Monopoly: MR = MC, then find P from demand.
22. For a monopolist, MR is less than P because:
✅ Correct Answer: B
To sell more output, a monopolist must lower the price on ALL units sold, not just the additional unit. This is why MR < P.

Example: If a monopolist sells 4 units at $8 each, MR of the 5th unit is only $3 (not $7) because the price on the first 4 units must be lowered to $7.
23. The Lerner Index measures:
✅ Correct Answer: B
The Lerner Index = (P − MC)/P = 1/|ε|. It measures the markup over marginal cost as a percentage of price.

Rule: Higher Lerner Index = More market power.
24. Student discounts are an example of:
✅ Correct Answer: C
Student discounts are third-degree price discrimination because they're based on observable group characteristics (student status).

Degrees: 1st = each customer; 2nd = quantity/version; 3rd = group characteristics.
25. Cartels are unstable because:
✅ Correct Answer: B
Cartels are unstable because each individual firm has an incentive to cheat by producing more than its agreed quantity, capturing a larger market share and higher profit.

Key insight: Cartels are profitable but unstable → they often collapse.
