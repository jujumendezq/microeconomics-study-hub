📖 Glossary of Key Terms

All important microeconomics terminology in one place

🔍 Search Glossary

A B C D E F G I L M N O P R S T U V W Z

A

Accounting Cost
Explicit, direct monetary costs (rent, materials, wages).
Accounting Profit
Total revenue minus explicit costs only.
Actuarial (Fair) Insurance
Insurance with premium exactly equal to the expected loss.
Antitrust Legislation
Laws that prohibit collusion and other anti-competitive practices.
Arc Elasticity
Another name for the midpoint method; elasticity calculated over an arc of the demand curve.
Atomicity
The assumption of many small firms, none with market power.
Average Cost (AC)
Total cost divided by quantity produced; cost per unit.
Average Fixed Cost (AFC)
Fixed cost divided by quantity; always declines as q increases.
Average Product (AP)
Total output divided by total labor; output per worker on average.
Average Variable Cost (AVC)
Variable cost divided by quantity.

B

Barriers to Entry
Factors that prevent new firms from entering a market.
Best Response
The strategy that maximizes a player's payoff given what others are doing.
Binding
When a price control actually affects the market (ceiling below equilibrium; floor above equilibrium).
Break-Even Price
The minimum price needed to cover all costs in the long run; equals minimum AC.

C

Cartel
A formal agreement between firms to cooperate on prices or output.
Causality
One variable directly causing a change in another.
Certainty Equivalent
The amount of money with certainty that gives the same utility as a gamble.
Ceteris Paribus
"All else equal" — assumption that other variables are held constant.
Club Good
Excludable and non‑rivalrous (cable TV, toll roads).
Coase Theorem
Private bargaining leads to efficient outcome if property rights clear and transaction costs low.
Cobb-Douglas
A specific production function: q = A × Kα × Lβ.
Collusion
When firms secretly cooperate to maximize joint profits.
Common Resource
Non‑excludable and rivalrous (fisheries, forests, water).
Complements
Goods used together (coffee & sugar, cars & gasoline).
Concave Function
A function that curves downward (flattens out), like the production function with diminishing returns.
Confounding Variable
A third variable that explains the relationship between two others.
Constant Returns to Scale (CRS)
Output doubles when inputs double.
Consumer Surplus (CS)
The difference between what buyers are willing to pay and the actual market price.
Control Group
The group that doesn't receive the treatment in an experiment.
Copyright
Government-granted rights to the creator of literary or artistic work.
Correlation
Two variables moving together.
Cost of Production
The total cost incurred by a producer to produce a good; represented by the area under the supply curve.
Counterfactual
What would have happened in the absence of an event.
Cournot-Nash Equilibrium
The Nash equilibrium in a quantity-setting duopoly.
Cross-Price Elasticity of Demand
The percentage change in quantity demanded of one good resulting from a 1% increase in the price of another good.

D

Deadweight Loss (DWL)
The net loss in total surplus from a market intervention.
Decreasing Returns to Scale (DRS)
Output less than doubles when inputs double.
Deflation
A general decrease in the level of prices.
Demand
Quantity buyers are willing and able to purchase at various prices.
Deviation / Cheating
When a firm breaks a collusive agreement to increase its own profit.
Difference-in-Differences
Comparing changes over time between treatment and control groups.
Diminishing Marginal Product
The tendency for the additional output from each extra unit of input to eventually decrease.
Direct Tax
Tax levied on individual income/wealth (depends on financial situation).
Diversification
A strategy to reduce risk by combining investments or activities that are not perfectly correlated.
Dominant Strategy
A strategy that gives the highest payoff regardless of what others do.
Duopoly
An oligopoly with exactly two firms.

E

Economic Cost
The sum of explicit and implicit costs.
Economic Incidence
Who actually bears the burden of the tax (depends on elasticities).
Economic Model
Simplified representation of reality used to understand relationships.
Economic Profit
Revenue minus economic costs (accounting + opportunity costs).
Economic Rent
The difference between benefit and economic cost.
Economics
Study of how people make choices under conditions of scarcity.
Economies of Scale
When the average cost of production decreases as output increases.
Efficiency
Maximizing total surplus; making the pie as big as possible.
Elastic Demand
|ε| > 1: Quantity changes more than proportionally to price.
Elasticity
Measure of responsiveness of one variable to changes in another; the percentage change in one variable resulting from a 1% increase in another.
Elasticity Rule
The more inelastic side bears more burden (taxes) or captures more benefit (subsidies).
Engel's Laws
Three observations about how spending patterns change with income: food share falls, housing/clothing share is constant, education/health/leisure share rises.
Equilibrium
A situation where no one can improve by changing behavior unilaterally.
Equity
Fairness in the distribution of resources; how the pie is divided.
Excess Demand (Shortage)
QD > QS at a given price.
Excess Supply (Surplus)
QS > QD at a given price.
Excludable
You can prevent people from using the good.
Explicit Costs
Monetary outflows (wages, rent, materials).
Extensive Margin
Adjustment in the number of firms (entry/exit).
Externalities
Costs or benefits affecting people not directly involved in a transaction.

F

Fair Lottery
A gamble with an expected value of zero.
Firm
An agent that produces goods/services; transforms inputs into outputs.
First-Degree Price Discrimination
Charging each customer their maximum willingness to pay.
Fixed Cost (FC)
Costs that don't vary with output.
Free Entry
No barriers preventing new firms from entering the market.
Free-Rider Problem
People enjoy benefits without paying → underprovision.

G

Game Theory
The study of strategic decision-making.
Gini Coefficient
Statistical measure of income inequality (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality).
Government Revenue
The tax money collected by the government.

I

Implicit Costs
Opportunity costs (foregone income, foregone interest).
Incentive
Something that motivates a particular action or behavior.
Income Elasticity of Demand
The percentage change in quantity demanded resulting from a 1% increase in income.
Increasing Returns to Scale (IRS)
Output more than doubles when inputs double.
Indirect Tax
A tax on units of goods or services, not dependent on income.
Individual Transferable Quotas (ITQs)
Allocating fishing rights to individuals.
Inelastic Demand
|ε| < 1: Quantity changes less than proportionally to price.
Inferior Good
Income ↑ → Demand ↓ (e.g., public transit, junk food).
Inflation
A general increase in the level of prices.
Innovation
An improvement that allows more output from the same inputs or the same output from fewer inputs.
Intensive Margin
Adjustment in quantity by existing firms.

L

Law of Demand
Inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.
Law of Supply
Direct relationship between price and quantity supplied.
Lerner Index
Measure of market power: (P − MC)/P = 1/|ε|.
Long Run
A period in which all inputs can be varied.
Luxury Good
A normal good where demand increases more than proportionally to income (εRD > 1).

M

Macroeconomics
Study of the economy as a whole, using aggregate measures.
Marginal Benefit
Additional benefit from consuming/producing one more unit.
Marginal Cost (MC)
Additional cost from consuming/producing one more unit.
Marginal Product (MP)
The additional output produced when one more unit of labor is employed.
Marginal Revenue (MR)
Additional revenue from selling one more unit.
Market
Group of buyers and sellers of a good or service.
Market Failure
When markets don't achieve optimal outcomes on their own.
Market Power
The ability of a firm to influence the market price.
Markup
The difference between price and marginal cost (P − MC).
Microeconomics
Study of individual economic agents and their interactions.
Midpoint Method
A method for calculating elasticity between two points that gives the same result regardless of direction.
Minimum Wage
A price floor on labor (wages).
Monopoly
A single seller in a market.
Monopsony
A market with only one buyer (market power on the demand side).
Movement Along the Curve
Change in quantity due to price change.

N

Nash Equilibrium
A situation where no player can improve by unilaterally changing strategy.
Natural Experiment
Real-world event that mimics a controlled experiment.
Natural Monopoly
Market where one firm can produce at lower cost than multiple firms.
Necessity Good
A normal good where demand increases less than proportionally to income (0 < εRD < 1).
Negative Externality
An externality that imposes a cost on others.
Network Effects
When the value of a product increases as more people use it.
Non-Binding
When a price control has no effect (ceiling above equilibrium; floor below equilibrium).
Non‑excludable
You cannot prevent people from using the good.
Non‑rivalrous
One person's use does not reduce availability for others.
Normal Good
Income ↑ → Demand ↑ (e.g., restaurant meals, vacations).
Normal Profit
Zero economic profit—just covers opportunity costs.
Normative Analysis
Subjective, value-based analysis of "what should be."

O

Oligopoly
Market with a few large firms that are interdependent.
Omitted Variable Bias
When an unobserved variable affects both X and Y, creating a spurious correlation.
Opportunity Cost
Value of the best alternative forgone when making a choice.
Ostrom
Nobel laureate; showed community management of common resources can work.
Overproduction
Producing more than the socially optimal quantity.
Own-Price Elasticity of Demand
The percentage change in quantity demanded resulting from a 1% increase in the good's own price.

P

Pareto Optimal
A situation where no one can be made better off without making someone else worse off.
Pareto Superior
One situation is Pareto superior if it makes at least one person better off without making anyone worse off.
Patent
Government-granted permission to be the sole producer of an invention.
Payoff Matrix
A table showing payoffs for all strategy combinations.
Payoffs
The outcomes for each combination of strategies.
Perfect Competition
Market structure with price takers, homogeneous goods, perfect info, free entry.
Perfectly Elastic Demand
|ε| = ∞: Any price change causes quantity to drop to zero (horizontal demand curve).
Perfectly Inelastic Demand
|ε| = 0: Quantity doesn't change with price (vertical demand curve).
Pigouvian Tax
Tax to correct negative externalities.
Players
The decision-makers in a game.
Pooling
A form of diversification where many independent risks are combined; used by insurance companies.
Positive Analysis
Objective, testable analysis of "what is."
Positive Externality
An externality that provides a benefit to others.
Price Ceiling
A legal maximum price that can be charged. Binding if set below equilibrium.
Price Discrimination
Selling the same good at different prices to different customers.
Price Elasticity of Supply
The percentage change in quantity supplied resulting from a 1% increase in price.
Price Floor
A legal minimum price that can be charged. Binding if set above equilibrium.
Price Maker
A firm that can influence the market price.
Price Taker
A firm that cannot influence the market price; must accept it.
Prisoner's Dilemma
A game showing why rational individuals might fail to cooperate even when cooperation makes everyone better off.
Private Good
Excludable and rivalrous (food, clothing, cars).
Producer Surplus (PS)
The difference between the market price and the seller's willingness to sell (cost of production).
Production Function
The relationship between inputs and the maximum output that can be produced given current technology.
Production Frontier
The boundary between possible and impossible production; points on the frontier are efficient.
Profit Maximization
The firm's goal: choose q to maximize π = TR − TC.
Protectionism
Government actions that restrict international trade.
Public Good
Non‑excludable and non‑rivalrous (national defense, streetlights).

R

Random Variable
A variable whose future value is uncertain.
Rationality
The assumption that people make choices to maximize their well-being.
Regulation
Government intervention to correct market failures.
Rent Control
A price ceiling on rental housing.
Representative Firm
A "typical" firm used to analyze the behavior of all firms.
Residual Demand
The demand a firm faces after competitors have supplied their output.
Returns to Scale
How output changes when ALL inputs are increased proportionally.
Reverse Causality
When Y causes X, rather than X causing Y.
Risk Premium
The maximum amount a risk-averse person will pay to eliminate risk.
Risk-Averse
Prefers certainty over a gamble with the same EV; concave utility function.
Risk-Loving
Prefers a gamble over certainty with the same EV; convex utility function.
Risk-Neutral
Indifferent between certainty and a gamble with the same EV; linear utility.
Rivalrous
One person's use reduces availability for others.

S

Scarcity
The fundamental economic problem: limited resources, unlimited wants.
Second-Degree Price Discrimination
Price varies by quantity or version (bulk discounts, economy vs. first class).
Short Run
A period in which at least one input is fixed.
Short Side of the Market
In disequilibrium, the smaller of QD and QS determines trade.
Shortage (Excess Demand)
Occurs when quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied.
Shutdown Price
The minimum price needed to cover variable costs; equals minimum AVC.
Shift of the Curve
Change in demand/supply due to other factors.
Social Optimum
The quantity where MSB = MSC.
Spurious Correlation
A correlation that doesn't reflect a causal relationship.
Statutory Incidence
Who is legally required to pay the tax to the government.
Strategies
The possible actions available to each player.
Subsidy
Government payment to encourage production or consumption; a negative tax.
Substitutes
Goods used in place of each other (coffee & tea).
Sunk Cost
Costs already incurred that can't be recovered.
Supply
Quantity sellers are willing and able to sell at various prices.
Supply Curve
Relationship between price and quantity supplied; MC above minimum AVC.
Surplus (Excess Supply)
Occurs when quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.
Systematic Risk
Risk that cannot be diversified away (affects all investments).

T

Tariff
A tax on imported goods.
Tax Incidence
How the burden of a tax is shared between buyers and sellers.
Tax Wedge
The gap between what buyers pay and what sellers receive (PB − PS).
Third-Degree Price Discrimination
Charging different prices to identifiable groups.
Total Cost (TC)
Sum of fixed and variable costs (FC + VC).
Total Revenue (TR)
Price × Quantity; the total amount received from selling a good.
Total Surplus (TS)
The sum of consumer surplus and producer surplus; CS + PS.
Trade-off
Giving up one thing to get another.
Tragedy of the Commons
Overuse of common resources (overfishing, groundwater depletion).
Transaction Costs
The costs of making an economic exchange.
Transfer
A payment from one group to another with no net loss to society.
Treatment Group
The group that receives the experimental treatment.

U

Underproduction
Producing less than the socially optimal quantity.
Unit-Elastic Demand
|ε| = 1: Quantity changes proportionally to price.
Utility Function of Wealth
A mathematical relationship that maps wealth levels to satisfaction levels.

V

Variable Cost (VC)
Costs that change with output level.
Vertical Summation
Adding willingness to pay across individuals for public goods.

W

Welfare
The well-being of society (consumer + producer surplus).
Welfare Analysis
Using CS and PS to measure the winners and losers from a policy.
Willingness to Pay (WTP)
The maximum amount a buyer is willing to pay for a good.
Willingness to Sell (WTS)
The minimum amount a seller is willing to accept for a good.

Z

Zero-Profit Condition
Long-run equilibrium where P = AC and economic profit = 0.
