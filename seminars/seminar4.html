🧑‍🏫 Seminar 4: Cost Curves, Firm Behavior, and Market Equilibrium

Understanding where supply curves come from

📌 Learning Objectives

  • Define and differentiate between the five key cost concepts: Fixed Cost (FC), Variable Cost (VC), Total Cost (TC), Average Cost (AC), and Marginal Cost (MC)
  • Explain the relationship between Marginal Cost and Average Cost—and why MC always intersects AC at its minimum
  • Calculate average product, marginal product, total cost, average cost, and marginal cost from given data
  • Identify the profit-maximizing output level using the MC = MR rule
  • Distinguish between the breakeven point (MC = AC) and the shutdown point (MC = AVC)
  • Explain how an increase in input costs shifts cost curves and supply curves
  • Solve for long-run equilibrium in a perfectly competitive market (price, quantity per firm, market quantity, and number of firms)
  • Interpret cost curve graphs and identify where firms make profit, break even, or shut down

🔹 Topic Introduction

Welcome to Seminar 4! This is where microeconomics gets really practical. Up until now, you've learned about supply, demand, and market equilibrium from a bird's-eye view. But here's the million-dollar question: Where do supply curves actually come from?

The answer is firms—and specifically, their costs. This seminar reveals the internal mechanics of firms: how much it costs them to produce things, how they decide how much to produce, and when they should shut down or leave the market entirely.

Think of it this way:
  • Consumer theory (earlier) explained demand—why people buy what they buy.
  • Cost theory (this seminar) explains supply—why firms sell what they sell.

🔹 Fixed Cost (FC)

Definition: Fixed costs are costs that do not change when the firm changes its output level. They exist even when the firm produces zero units.

Real-World Example: Imagine you open a pizza restaurant. You sign a lease for $2,000/month, buy an oven for $10,000, and get a business license for $500/year. Whether you sell 0 pizzas or 1,000 pizzas, these costs remain the same. That's your fixed cost.
⚠️ Key Points:
  • FC > 0 for most businesses
  • FC is independent of output (q)
  • FC must be paid even if q = 0
  • FC = TC when q = 0

🔹 Variable Cost (VC)

Definition: Variable costs are costs that change when the firm changes its output level. They increase as production increases.

Real-World Example: In your pizza restaurant, variable costs include:
  • Flour, cheese, and toppings (you need more as you make more pizzas)
  • Hourly wages for delivery drivers
  • Electricity for the oven (the more you cook, the more energy you use)
⚠️ Key Points:
  • VC = 0 when q = 0
  • VC increases as q increases
  • VC is the portion of TC that depends on output

🔹 Total Cost (TC)

Definition: Total Cost is the sum of Fixed Cost and Variable Cost. It represents the total amount the firm must pay to produce a given quantity.

TC = FC + VC
Real-World Example: Your pizza restaurant's total cost for producing 100 pizzas:
  • FC = $2,000 (rent + equipment depreciation)
  • VC = $500 (ingredients + wages)
  • TC = $2,500

🔹 Average Cost (AC)

Definition: Average Cost is the cost per unit of output. It tells you, on average, how much it costs to produce each unit.

AC = TC / q
Real-World Example: Your pizza restaurant produces 100 pizzas at TC = $2,500. AC = $2,500 / 100 = $25 per pizza.
AC = AFC + AVC

where AFC = FC/q and AVC = VC/q

🔹 Average Variable Cost (AVC)

Definition: Average Variable Cost is the variable cost per unit of output.

AVC = VC / q
⚠️ Key Insight: AVC is crucial for the shutdown decision. If the price falls below AVC, the firm can't even cover its variable costs and should shut down immediately.

🔹 Marginal Cost (MC)

Definition: Marginal Cost is the cost of producing one additional unit of output. It tells you how much TC increases when you produce one more unit.

MC = ΔTC / Δq
Real-World Example: Your pizza restaurant is producing 100 pizzas at TC = $2,500. If you produce a 101st pizza, TC rises to $2,535. MC = $35 for that 101st pizza.
⚠️ Key Insight: MC is the most important cost concept for economists. Why? Because firms "think at the margin"—they decide whether to produce one more unit based on whether the additional revenue exceeds the additional cost.

🔹 Returns to Scale

Definition: Returns to scale describe how output changes when all inputs are increased by a certain proportion.

TypeDefinitionRelationship
Increasing Returns to ScaleOutput increases more than proportionally to inputsIf inputs double, output > doubles
Constant Returns to ScaleOutput increases proportionally to inputsIf inputs double, output exactly doubles
Decreasing Returns to ScaleOutput increases less than proportionally to inputsIf inputs double, output < doubles

🔹 Marginal Product (MP) and Average Product (AP)

Marginal Product (MP) = The additional output produced by hiring one more worker.

Average Product (AP) = Total output divided by the number of workers.

MP = Δq / ΔL
AP = q / L
⚠️ Key Relationship: There's an inverse relationship between MP and MC:
  • High MP → low MC (productive workers reduce costs)
  • Low MP → high MC (unproductive workers increase costs)

Key Insight: MC intersects AC and AVC at their minimum points. When MC < AC, AC is falling. When MC > AC, AC is rising. MC is the "leader" that pulls AC and AVC up or down.

🔹 Breakeven Point

Definition: The breakeven point is when Total Revenue = Total Cost (profit = 0). At this point, price equals Average Cost (P = AC).

Real-World Example: Your pizza restaurant has AC = $25 per pizza. If the market price is $25, you're breaking even—you're covering all costs but making no profit. If the price is $30, you make profit. If the price is $20, you make a loss.
Breakeven point: MC = AC (at minimum AC)

🔹 Shutdown Point

Definition: The shutdown point is when price equals Average Variable Cost (P = AVC). If price falls below AVC, the firm should shut down immediately.

Shutdown point: MC = AVC (at minimum AVC)
⚠️ Key Rules:
  • P < AVC → Shut down (can't cover variable costs)
  • AVC < P < AC → Operate at a loss (cover variable costs plus some fixed costs)
  • P = AC → Break even
  • P > AC → Make profit

📈 Graph: The Firm's Supply Decision

Figure 2: The Firm's Supply Decision
Short-run supply is MC above minimum AVC

Key Insight: The firm's supply curve is the portion of the MC curve above the minimum AVC. Below this point, the firm shuts down and supplies zero.

🧮 Key Formulas

AP = q / L (Average Product)
MP = Δq / ΔL (Marginal Product)
TC = FC + VC (Total Cost)
AC = TC / q (Average Cost)
AVC = VC / q (Average Variable Cost)
MC = ΔTC / Δq (Marginal Cost)
Profit Maximization: P = MC (in perfect competition)
Breakeven: P = AC
Shutdown: P = AVC
Profit: π = (P − AC) × q
Market Supply: QS = n × q(P)

📝 Exam Scenarios & Problem-Solving

Scenario 1: Computing Marginal Product

Scenario: L = 0, q = 0; L = 1, q = 20; L = 2, q = 50; L = 3, q = 120.

Solution: AP = q/L: 20, 25, 40. MP = Δq/ΔL: 20, 30, 70. MP is increasing → increasing marginal returns.

Scenario 2: Input Cost Increase

Scenario: An increase in input costs will result in:

Solution: All three statements are correct: leftward shift in firm's supply, upward shift of MC, leftward shift of market supply.

Scenario 3: Reconstructing Costs

Scenario: q=1: AC=60, MC=20; q=2: AC=45, MC=40; q=3: AC=47, MC=60; q=4: AC=53, MC=80.

Solution: TC = AC×q. FC = TC − VC. For q=1: VC=20, FC=40. Fixed cost is 40.

Scenario 4: Breakeven and Shutdown Points

Scenario: TC(q) = 0.25q² + 8q + 16.

Solution: MC = 0.5q + 8, ATC = 0.25q + 8 + 16/q. Breakeven: q = 8. Shutdown: q = 0 (no positive shutdown point).

Scenario 5: Long-Run Equilibrium

Scenario: TC(q) = 16 + q², market demand QD = 24 − P. Find long-run equilibrium.

Solution: P = MC = 2q, P = AC = 16/q + q. Set 2q = 16/q + q → q = 4. P = 8. QD = 24 − 8 = 16. n = 16/4 = 4 firms.

Scenario 6: Profit Calculation

Scenario: TC = 500 + 0.25Q², P = 50. Find profit.

Solution: MC = 0.5Q. Set P = MC → 50 = 0.5Q → Q = 100. TC = 500 + 0.25(10000) = 3000. TR = 50 × 100 = 5000. π = 2000.

Common Exam Traps

  • Confusing AP and MP: AP = q/L, MP = Δq/ΔL.
  • Confusing MC and AC: P = MC is profit-maximizing; P = AC is breakeven.
  • Confusing shutdown and breakeven: Shutdown is P < AVC, not P < AC.
  • Confusing shifts and movements: Price changes cause movements; other factors shift the curve.
  • Forgetting to square in TC: 2Q² means Q² × 2.
  • Using AC instead of MC: Profit maximization is P = MC.

📝 Practice Questions

Q1: A firm's fixed costs are $100, variable costs are $50 for producing 2 units. What is total cost?
✅ Correct Answer: C
TC = FC + VC = $100 + $50 = $150
Q2: When MC < AC, the AC curve is:
✅ Correct Answer: B
When MC < AC, producing one more unit pulls the average down, so AC is falling.
Q3: A firm should shut down in the short run when:
✅ Correct Answer: B
The firm should shut down if it cannot cover its variable costs (P < AVC).
Q4: In long-run equilibrium in perfect competition:
✅ Correct Answer: B
In long-run equilibrium with free entry and exit, firms earn zero economic profits.
Q5: Marginal cost is the:
✅ Correct Answer: C
Marginal cost is the cost of producing one additional unit: MC = ΔTC/Δq.

📖 Glossary

Fixed Cost (FC)
Costs that don't change with output.
Variable Cost (VC)
Costs that change with output.
Total Cost (TC)
Sum of fixed and variable costs.
Average Cost (AC)
Cost per unit of output.
Average Variable Cost (AVC)
Variable cost per unit.
Marginal Cost (MC)
Cost of producing one more unit.
Average Product (AP)
Output per worker.
Marginal Product (MP)
Additional output from one more worker.
Breakeven Point
Point where profit = 0 (P = AC = MC).
Shutdown Point
Point where firm stops producing (P = AVC = MC).
Profit Maximization
Producing where additional revenue equals additional cost (P = MC).
Returns to Scale
How output responds when all inputs increase.
Perfect Competition
Market with many firms selling identical goods.
Price Taker
Firm that can't influence the market price.
Supply Curve
Portion of MC above AVC.
