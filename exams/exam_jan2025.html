Question 1

Among the following statements, which one is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: C
Producer surplus is the area above the supply curve and below the market price. Consumer surplus is the area below the demand curve and above the price.

Question 2

As part of a randomized experiment, Jessica Cohen and Pascaline Dupas created an NGO to distribute mosquito nets in prenatal care centers in Kenya. They distributed these mosquito nets at different prices, randomly assigned to different centers. They found that the purchase of a mosquito net was heavily dependent on price. When mosquito nets were offered for a price close to zero, almost all households in a village (400 out of 500) took them home. However, with a marginal increase in price, demand dropped drastically, with only 20 households out of 500 purchasing the nets in another village. What can be said about the price elasticity of demand \(\epsilon_{P}\) of mosquito nets in Kenya?
✅ Correct Answer: D
A small price increase caused a large drop in quantity demanded (from 400 to 20). This indicates that demand is very elastic. \(|\epsilon_P| > 1\).

Question 3

In The Evolution of Cooperation (1984), Robert Axelrod explores how countries often face a "prisoner's dilemma" during an arms race. In this scenario, two nations (players) must choose whether to arm themselves (defect) or disarm (cooperate). If both countries disarm (cooperate), both avoid the high costs of maintaining large arsenals, leading to a peaceful and mutually beneficial outcome. If one country arms while the other disarms, the arming country gains a strategic military advantage, leaving the disarming country vulnerable. If both countries arm (defect), they incur massive costs maintaining weapons, creating a situation where both lose resources despite no actual conflict occurring. The normal form of the game is given below: (Matrix: Arm, Arm: 2,2; Arm, Disarm: 4,1; Disarm, Arm: 1,4; Disarm, Disarm: 3,3). Note that payoffs are the net benefit for the country. Based on the above matrix, what can you conclude?
✅ Correct Answer: A
The dominant strategy is to arm (defect). (Arm, Arm) gives (2,2). If one country disarms while the other arms, the arming country gets 4, but the disarming country gets 1. So both have an incentive to arm. (Arm, Arm) is the Nash equilibrium, but it is not Pareto efficient because (Disarm, Disarm) gives (3,3), which is better for both.

Question 4

Question 5

Consider the market for handcrafted ceramic mugs. In a competitive market, each identical artisan studio faces the following total cost structure: \(TC(q) = 49 + q^2\). The market demand for handcrafted ceramic mugs is given by: \(Q_D(P) = 84 - P\). How many studios operate in the market if the industry reaches long-run equilibrium?
✅ Correct Answer: D
In long-run equilibrium, P = min ATC. ATC = 49/q + q. Min ATC: d(ATC)/dq = -49/q^2 + 1 = 0 → q = 7. ATC = 49/7 + 7 = 7 + 7 = 14. So P = 14. Market demand at P=14: Q = 84 - 14 = 70. Number of firms = 70 / 7 = 10.

Question 6

A company that manufactures surfboards pays each of its employees a salary of $1,500. The company's other variable costs amount to $300 per surfboard produced. The fixed cost is $1,000. If the company employs 4 workers and produces 25 surfboards, the average cost of the company is:
✅ Correct Answer: B
Total variable cost = 4*1500 + 25*300 = 6000 + 7500 = 13500. Total cost = 13500 + 1000 = 14500. Average cost = 14500 / 25 = 580.

Question 7

Cigarette consumption leads to increased healthcare costs due to hospitalization from smoking-related diseases. The government has decided to impose a production tax on cigarette manufacturers to account for the externality and reach an economically efficient outcome. The optimal production tax is equal to:
✅ Correct Answer: A
The optimal Pigouvian tax is equal to the external cost, which is the difference between the social marginal cost (SMC) and the private marginal cost (PMC). Tax = SMC - PMC.

Question 8

Question 9

Assume that a single caterer manages all cafeterias of a Swiss university, granting it a monopoly status within all its buildings. The demand function for cafeteria meals is given by: \(Q_{D} = 20 - P\) and the marginal cost of the caterer is given by \(MC = 2Q\). Which of the following propositions regarding the introduction of a price ceiling is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: D
Monopoly: Demand: P = 20 - Q. MR = 20 - 2Q. MR = MC → 20 - 2Q = 2Q → Q = 5, P = 15. A price ceiling of 15 is exactly the monopoly price, so it has no impact.

Question 10

The demand function for tennis rackets is as follows: \(Q_{D} = 300 - 4P + 10P_{P}\) where \(P\) is the price of a tennis racket, and \(P_{P}\) is the price of a padel (a sport similar to tennis) racket. The current price of a tennis racket is 50 CHF, and the price of a padel racket is 10 CHF. At these price levels, what is the cross-price elasticity of demand for tennis rackets with respect to the price of padel rackets?
✅ Correct Answer: A
Cross-price elasticity = (dQ/dPp) * (Pp/Q). dQ/dPp = 10. Q = 300 - 4*50 + 10*10 = 300 - 200 + 100 = 200. Elasticity = 10 * (10/200) = 10 * 0.05 = 0.5.

Question 14

Consider the market for photo editing, operating under conditions of perfect competition. The market is characterized by the following supply and demand functions: \(Q_{S}(P) = 2P - 6\) and \(Q_{D}(P) = 10 - 2P\). Recently, new AI-enhanced photo editing softwares led to a significant reduction in editing costs. This modified the supply function, which is now: \(Q_{S}(P) = 2P - 2\). What is the effect on market equilibrium?
✅ Correct Answer: B
Initial equilibrium: 2P - 6 = 10 - 2P → 4P = 16 → P = 4, Q = 2. Revenue = 8. New equilibrium: 2P - 2 = 10 - 2P → 4P = 12 → P = 3, Q = 4. Revenue = 12.

Question 15

The platform economy, driven by digitalisation, includes digital labour platforms that connect workers to jobs. Location-based platforms, like Uber, offer flexible schedules for workers, while online platforms, like Upwork, enable tasks such as graphic design to be completed remotely, allowing workers to work from anywhere. The number of platform workers has surged, especially post-COVID, when more people started looking for flexible work. The International Labour Office (ILO) estimates that digital platform workers make up 9.7% of Europe's workforce (ILO 2021). However, despite this growth, many platform workers have seen their wages decline, and their jobs have become less secure. In a market for labor where price is the wage paid to workers (supply) by employers (demand), what can explain the situation?
✅ Correct Answer: D
An increase in the number of platform workers (rightward shift of labor supply) with unchanged demand leads to lower wages and higher unemployment (oversupply).

Question 16

Question 17

Question 18

In an article published in 2019 in the Journal Nature, authors warn that the capacity of groundwater to replenish itself has been completely exceeded by the rate of water abstraction, 70% of which goes to agriculture. Question 19

In 2017, a new AI-based price-setting software was introduced in the gasoline market in Germany. Unlike older systems, it learns from its past experiences and allows for quick price adjustments, taking into account the prices set by other stations to maximize profits. A recent study showed that, in oligopolistic situations, this software increases profits when all stations use it. What is the main economic effect observed by the researchers?
✅ Correct Answer: A
The AI software enables tacit collusion (an informal cartel), allowing firms to coordinate on higher prices, moving the market closer to monopoly pricing.

Question 20

Question 21

Ocevne, a singer from Geneva, wants to release her new album on Spotify. She has two options: I. Sign a contract with a label that takes a commission of one-third of the revenue generated by streams on Spotify. In return, the label will use its network to add some of her songs to playlists to boost the album's visibility. II. Use Distrokid, an online service that allows her to release the album without taking any commission on the revenue generated by streams on Spotify but will not do any promotion. The table below presents the probabilities associated with different volumes of streams for each option: (Label: 10k-0.3, 50k-0.3, 1M-0.3, 50M-0.1; Distrokid: 10k-0.5, 50k-0.35, 1M-0.1, 50M-0.05). Knowing that Ocevne is risk-neutral and that 1,000 Spotify streams generate 3 CHF in revenue, which option will Ocevne choose and why?
✅ Correct Answer: A
Expected revenue (label) = 0.3*(10,000/1000*3) + 0.3*(50,000/1000*3) + 0.3*(1,000,000/1000*3) + 0.1*(50,000,000/1000*3) = 0.3*30 + 0.3*150 + 0.3*3000 + 0.1*150000 = 9 + 45 + 900 + 15000 = 15954. After 1/3 commission: 15954 * (2/3) = 10636. Expected revenue (Distrokid) = 0.5*30 + 0.35*150 + 0.1*3000 + 0.05*150000 = 15 + 52.5 + 300 + 7500 = 7867.5. So the label has a higher expected value.

Question 22

After the merger of WarnerMedia and Discovery in April 2022, the combined company has access to a vast library of content, including streaming platforms like HBO Max and Discovery+. Suppose the merged company implements a pricing strategy where it charges different prices for streaming subscriptions based on the geographical location of users. Question 23

In the town of Greentown, a paper mill produces high-quality paper but discharges wastewater into a river, causing environmental damage with an associated marginal social cost given by \(MSC = Q + 4\). The demand function for the high-quality paper is given by \(Q_D = 50 - P\), while the supply function is \(Q_S = P\). Which of the following propositions is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: A
Private equilibrium: Q = 50 - P, Q = P → 2Q = 50 → Q = 25, P = 25. Social optimum: MSC = Q + 4, Demand: P = 50 - Q. Set 50 - Q = Q + 4 → 46 = 2Q → Q = 23. Reduction = 2 units.

Question 24

A pharmaceutical company holds a monopoly over the production of a life-saving drug due to patent protection. The company must decide on the optimal pricing and production strategy to maximize its profits while considering market demand and production costs. Which of the following propositions is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: C
A monopolist maximizes profit by producing where MR = MC. This is the standard profit-maximizing condition for all firms.

Question 25

Consider a perfectly competitive market characterized by the following demand and supply functions: \(Q_{D} = 100 - 2P\) and \(Q_{S} = 5P - 50\). The government introduces a per-unit tax of 5. Calculate the total surplus after the tax is imposed. Which of the following answers is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: D
Initial equilibrium: 100 - 2P = 5P - 50 → 150 = 7P → P ≈ 21.43, Q ≈ 57.14. With tax: Supply becomes Qs = 5(P - 5) - 50 = 5P - 75. New equilibrium: 100 - 2P = 5P - 75 → 175 = 7P → P = 25 (consumer). Q = 100 - 50 = 50. CS = 0.5 * 50 * (50 - 25) = 625. PS = 0.5 * 50 * (20 - 10) = 250. Tax revenue = 5 * 50 = 250. Total surplus = 625 + 250 + 250 = 1125.

Question 28

Alpine-electricity is the sole provider of electricity to a remote village in the Alps, where the demand for electricity in kilowatts per hour (kWh) is the following: \(Q_{D} = 1000 - P\). The marginal cost of Alpine-electricity is constant and equal to 100 CHF/kWh. What can be inferred from this?
✅ Correct Answer: C
Monopoly: Demand: P = 1000 - Q. MR = 1000 - 2Q. MR = MC → 1000 - 2Q = 100 → 2Q = 900 → Q = 450. P = 1000 - 450 = 550.

Question 29

To try to limit over-tourism without putting a limit on the number of tourists allowed to enter the city, Venice has begun charging for access to its city center on high-traffic tourist days. This measure is aimed at countering the damage caused by tourists, who are partly responsible for the city's deterioration and the pollution of its waters. In particular, tourists will have to download a QR code for 5 euros from a dedicated website to be able to enter the center. The purpose of the measure taken by the Venice municipality is:
✅ Correct Answer: B
The access fee is a Pigouvian tax designed to internalize the negative externality of over-tourism. It makes tourists pay for the damage they cause, discouraging excessive visits.
