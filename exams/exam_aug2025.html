Question 1

The government is considering implementing a Pigouvian subsidy to vaccine producers to internalize a positive externality associated with vaccines. Vaccinated individuals not only protect themselves from illness but also reduce the spread of disease, benefiting the community as large. Which of the following statements is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: A
A Pigouvian subsidy shifts the supply curve down, increasing the equilibrium quantity. The increase in quantity is larger when demand is more elastic because the subsidy causes a larger quantity response for a given price reduction.

Question 2

In an effort to reduce environmental waste, the government introduces a tax on disposable plastic packaging used by food retailers. The market is characterized by the demand function \(Q^{D} = 120 - 2P\) and the supply function \(Q^{S} = 4P - 20\) where \(Q\) is the quantity. \(P\) is the market price. The government's goal is to reduce the quantity exchanged by 30 units. Choose the correct answer:
✅ Correct Answer: C
Initially, equilibrium: 120 - 2P = 4P - 20 → 140 = 6P → P = 23.33, Q = 73.33. To reduce Q by 30, new Q = 43.33. Price paid by consumers: 120 - 2P = 43.33 → P = 38.33. Price received by producers: 4P - 20 = 43.33 → P = 15.83. Tax = 22.5. Deadweight loss = 0.5 * 30 * 22.5 = 337.5.

Question 3

A small wheat farm in a rural region operates in a perfectly competitive agricultural market. The total cost of the farm is given by \(TC = 100 + 20q^2\). The farmer aims to maximize its profit. Which of the following statements is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: C
In the short run, a firm exits if P < AVC. The average variable cost (AVC) is TVC/q = (20q^2)/q = 20q. However, the correct shut-down condition in the long run is P < AC. AC = (100/q) + 20q. Option C is the correct expression for AC. A firm exits in the long run if P < AC. Option C represents the average total cost (ATC).

Question 4

Elizabeth received a promotion at work that increased her income by 20%. In the months following her promotion, Elizabeth increased her consumption of chocolate by 10% and cosmetics by 40%. Which of the following statements is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: C
Income elasticity of demand for chocolate = 10%/20% = 0.5 (normal, necessity). Income elasticity for cosmetics = 40%/20% = 2 (normal, luxury). Thus, chocolate is a necessity and cosmetics is a luxury good.

Question 5

To accelerate the transition to electrified road transport, China introduced various incentives to promote electric vehicles. Between 2009 and 2022, the country invested over 200 billion RMB ($29 billion) in subsidies and tax breaks to support electric vehicle (EV) production. While fewer than 10,000 electric vehicles were sold in China in 2009, that number soared to over 6 million by 2022, accounting for more than half of global EV sales. Assume that the production subsidy for EV did not move the demand curve, what is the potential impact of China's EV subsidy policy?
✅ Correct Answer: C
A production subsidy shifts the supply curve down (to the right). If neither supply nor demand is perfectly inelastic, the equilibrium quantity will increase. The price paid by consumers will decrease, and the price received by producers will increase.

Question 6

Which of the following situation describes third-degree price discrimination?
✅ Correct Answer: C
Third-degree price discrimination involves charging different prices to different groups of consumers based on observable characteristics (e.g., age, location, time of purchase). Movie theaters charging lower prices on Tuesday nights is a classic example.

Question 7

When the weather is hot, the shade of a small tree can be considered as what type of good?
✅ Correct Answer: A
The shade of a tree is non-rival (one person's enjoyment doesn't reduce another's) and non-excludable (it's difficult to prevent someone from enjoying the shade). This makes it a public good.

Question 9

By the late 20th century, digital cameras became more affordable and widely popular among consumers. CaptureX, a film camera company was slow to adapt to producing digital products. As digital camera prices dropped, the cost of producing film cameras went up relatively to that of digital cameras, making them less competitive. This caused CaptureX to lose market share and eventually exit from the consumer photography market. The figure below illustrates the sales of film-alone, film cameras, and stand-alone digital cameras over time. Which statement below is correct to explain what happened to CaptureX, assuming that the market is perfectly competitive?
✅ Correct Answer: B
In a perfectly competitive market, firms exit in the long run when P < AC (price is below average cost). CaptureX could not cover its average costs due to the decline in price, leading to its exit.

Question 10

According to the European Heat Pump Association, 765,000 heat pumps were sold in 13 European countries in the first six months of 2024, which is nearly half as many as the ones installed during the first six months of 2023. According to the spokesperson for the Swiss Heat Pump Association, the rise in gas prices due to the war in Ukraine explains this: "There was a bubble. What economic concepts best describes this statement?
✅ Correct Answer: C
Gas boilers and heat pumps are substitutes. The rise in gas prices increased the demand for heat pumps in 2023 (a bubble), as people moved their purchases forward. This explains the drop in 2024 sales.

Question 11

A local water utility company operating as a legal monopoly in a small town finds that its marginal revenue and marginal cost are both equal to 19 at 12 units of output. At this quantity the price on the demand curve is $40 and the average total cost is $11. What is the total profit earned?
✅ Correct Answer: B
Profit = Total Revenue - Total Cost. TR = P * Q = 40 * 12 = 480. TC = ATC * Q = 11 * 12 = 132. Profit = 480 - 132 = 348.

Question 12

Based on this data, which of the following statements best explains how supply and demand factors may have contributed to the observed long-term rise in real house prices?
✅ Correct Answer: B
A rightward shift in demand (e.g., due to population growth, urbanization, or increased wealth) combined with inelastic or limited supply would drive prices upward over time. This matches the observed trend.

Question 13

If it rains during the next harvest season, a farmer will have an income of CHF 24,000. On the other hand, if there is a drought, his income will only be CHF 12,000. The probability of rain during the next season is 75%, and the probability of a drought is 25%. If the farmer can insure against the risk of drought and thus obtain with certainty the expected value of his income, what would be the amount of the actuarially fair insurance premium (i.e., the premium equivalent to his expected loss)?
✅ Correct Answer: B
Expected income = 0.75 * 24000 + 0.25 * 12000 = 18000 + 3000 = 21000. Without insurance, the farmer's expected income is 21000. The actuarially fair premium is the expected loss: 0.25 * (24000 - 12000) = 0.25 * 12000 = 3000. So the premium is 3000.

Question 15

In the United States, over 67,000 accidents involving circular saws occur each year, about 4,000 of which result in amputations. The societal costs are estimated to exceed 2.3 billion USD. SawStop, a patented technology, detects skin and immediately stops the saw, reducing the risk of serious injuries to nearly zero. However, adopting this system involves relatively high costs for saw manufacturers, while consumers tend to underestimate the risk of accidents and are highly price-sensitive. No manufacturer has ended up adopting the technology. Which of the following statements best explains this situation?
✅ Correct Answer: B
The manufacturers do not bear the societal costs (the negative externality) of saw injuries. Since consumers are price-sensitive and undervalue the safety benefit, there is no market incentive for manufacturers to adopt the expensive technology.

Question 16

Switzerland used to impose import taxes in markets such as automobiles and household appliances. However, the Swiss Parliament voted to abolish these taxes effective January 1, 2024. Assuming that the markets for industrial goods are perfectly competitive, which of the following statements is true following the policy change?
✅ Correct Answer: D
Removing an import tax (tariff) in a perfectly competitive market shifts the supply curve down (or right), lowering the price paid by consumers and increasing the quantity exchanged. This leads to an increase in consumer surplus.

Question 17

Consider an arbitrary market. Which characteristic of the good or service leads to free-riding behavior by consumers (that is, which characteristic of this good or service enables people to enjoy consuming it even if they do not fully pay for it)?
✅ Correct Answer: B
Free-riding occurs when it is impossible to exclude non-payers from consuming the good. Non-excludability is the key characteristic that enables free-riding.

Question 18

The housing rental market in Geneva is defined by a demand function \(Q^D = 2000 - P\) and supply function \(Q^S = 0.25P + 200\), respectively. The market is initially in competitive equilibrium. Then the government imposes a price ceiling of 1000 CHF. Which of the following statements is true?
✅ Correct Answer: D
Initial equilibrium: 2000 - P = 0.25P + 200 → 1800 = 1.25P → P = 1440, Q = 560. With price ceiling at 1000, quantity supplied: Qs = 0.25*1000 + 200 = 450. Quantity demanded: Qd = 2000 - 1000 = 1000. The quantity exchanged is limited by supply: 450. Reduction = 560 - 450 = 110.

Question 20

High Price: Attract fewer customers but earn more profit per sale. The normal form of the game is given below (payoffs are profits). Question 21

Consider firms operating in a perfectly competitive environment. Which statement is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: D
A decrease in input costs lowers the marginal cost for firms, shifting their individual supply curves to the right (or down). The market supply curve, which is the horizontal summation of individual firm supply curves, also shifts to the right.
