Question 1

In a perfectly competitive market, which of the following statements is true in equilibrium?
✅ Correct Answer: C
In a perfectly competitive market equilibrium, total surplus (CS + PS) is maximized. This is the defining efficiency property of competitive markets.
A is wrong: CS and PS depend on the specific supply and demand curves.
B is wrong: Taxes decrease producer surplus (and consumer surplus).
D is wrong: Taxes decrease consumer surplus (and producer surplus).

Question 2

In the air travel market between Geneva and Barcelona, with significant pollution and negative environmental externalities. The demand function is QD = 40 − P, the supply function is QS = P, and the marginal social cost is MC = Q + 20. Which of the following statements is true?
✅ Correct Answer: D
Step 1: Find private equilibrium: 40 − P = P → P = 20, Q = 20
Step 2: Find social optimum: 40 − Q = Q + 20 → 2Q = 20 → Q* = 10
Result: A Pigouvian tax would reduce quantity from 20 to 10 → reduction of 10 flights.
A is wrong: Market price at social optimum is P = 30 (but that's the price after tax).
B is wrong: Subsidies are for positive externalities, not negative.
C is wrong: Subsidies would increase quantity further (wrong direction).

Question 3

Suppose that the market for cuckoo clocks is perfectly competitive. The marginal cost of each firm is given by MC = 12 + 4Q. If 60 firms operate in the market with this same marginal cost function, what will be the quantity supplied if the price is 20 CHF?
✅ Correct Answer: D
Step 1: Set P = MC: 20 = 12 + 4q → 8 = 4q → q = 2
Step 2: Each firm supplies 2 units.
Step 3: Market supply = 60 × 2 = 120
Key concept: In perfect competition, firm supplies where P = MC.

Question 4

In 2020, the Norwegian government introduced a temporary tax relief package for the Oil and Gas sector to cushion it from the consequences of a fall in global oil demand. Think of this policy as a subsidy provided to the firms to produce oil. Assuming that the supply of oil is upward sloping, which of the following statements is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: A
A subsidy benefits both producers and consumers when demand is not perfectly elastic or inelastic. The benefit is shared based on relative elasticities.
Key rule: The more inelastic side captures more of the subsidy benefit.
B is wrong: Consumers only capture all the benefit if demand is perfectly inelastic or supply is perfectly elastic.
C is wrong: If demand is perfectly inelastic, consumers capture all the benefit, not producers.

Question 5

In a second-hand bicycle market, merchants are selling 60 bicycles. Some bikes are of good quality, others are of poor quality. Buyers cannot observe the quality. If buyers are risk neutral, what can you say with certainty about the equilibrium outcome?
✅ Correct Answer: A
This is the classic adverse selection problem (Akerlof's "lemons" problem).
Calculations:
• Good quality: WTP = 1000, Reservation price = 800
• Bad quality: WTP = 400, Reservation price = 350
If buyers are risk neutral and cannot observe quality, the expected value of a bicycle is:
EV = q × 400 + (1−q) × 1000
The market will clear if EV ≥ 800 (sellers' reservation price for good quality).
q × 400 + (1−q) × 1000 ≥ 800 → q ≤ 1/3
If q > 1/3, good quality bicycles leave the market → adverse selection.
B is wrong: The threshold is q ≤ 1/3 (not 30 bicycles).
C is wrong: There will be adverse selection.

Question 6

Swiss cereal production not only feeds the population but also beautifies the country's landscape. Assume that the marginal external benefit is 4 CHF per kilogram and that the private demand and supply are QD = 5 − P and QS = 1 + 3P. By how many kilograms would the quantity traded increase if we move from the market equilibrium to the socially optimal quantity?
✅ Correct Answer: C
Step 1: Private equilibrium: 5 − P = 1 + 3P → 4 = 4P → P = 1, Q = 4
Step 2: Social optimum: MSB = MPB + External Benefit = (5 − Q) + 4 = 9 − Q
Set MSB = MC: 9 − Q = 1 + 3Q → 8 = 4Q → Q* = 6
Step 3: Increase = 6 − 4 = +3 kg
Key concept: Positive externality → market underproduces → subsidy increases quantity.

Question 8

Assume that the price elasticity of demand for an essential medicine is zero (demand is perfectly price-inelastic), and that supply has a standard shape. What will be the impact of a technological invention that allows cheaper production of this medicine on this market?
✅ Correct Answer: C
Step 1: Perfectly inelastic demand means the demand curve is vertical.
Step 2: Cheaper production shifts the supply curve RIGHT (down).
Step 3: With a vertical demand curve, the quantity exchanged remains the same, but the price decreases.
Key concept: When demand is perfectly inelastic, all the benefit of lower production costs goes to consumers in the form of lower prices.

Question 9

The figure shows the predictions of temperature variability and GDP per capita. What can we deduce from this diagram?
✅ Correct Answer: C
The diagram shows that countries with higher GDP per capita tend to have lower predicted temperature variability. This indicates a negative correlation between the two variables.
Important: Correlation does not imply causation! We cannot conclude that wealth causes lower temperature variability (D is wrong).

Question 10

Before the COVID-19 pandemic, the demand for protective masks was QD = 400 − 2P. With the onset of the pandemic, demand increased to QD = 700 − 2P. The supply of masks is QS = 100 + P. Compute the short-term change in consumer surplus caused by the shift in demand.
✅ Correct Answer: B
Step 1: Initial equilibrium: 400 − 2P = 100 + P → 300 = 3P → P = 100, Q = 200
Step 2: Initial CS: Demand intercept = 200 (when Q=0: P=200). CS = ½ × 200 × (200−100) = 10,000
Step 3: New equilibrium: 700 − 2P = 100 + P → 600 = 3P → P = 200, Q = 300
Step 4: New CS: Demand intercept = 350 (when Q=0: P=350). CS = ½ × 300 × (350−200) = 22,500
Step 5: Change = 22,500 − 10,000 = +12,500
